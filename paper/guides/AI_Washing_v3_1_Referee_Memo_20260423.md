# AI-Washing v3.1 Referee Memo

Date: 2026-04-23  
Purpose: compact referee-style memo after the `v3.1` market-identification package and non-market Packets A-C.

## 1. Bottom-Line Verdict

The paper is now more persuasive as a measurement-and-consequences paper than as a market-mispricing paper.

That is not a downgrade. It is a cleaner and more defendable contribution.

The strongest version of the paper now is:
- it builds and audits a scalable filing-based measure of AI-disclosure credibility;
- it shows that the measure predicts later technology-realization patterns;
- it shows that markets do not cleanly distinguish higher- and lower-credibility AI disclosure at filing or in stricter post-filing tests;
- it shows coherent later non-market consequences, especially lower profitability and higher R&D intensity at `t+2` within the AI-talking sample;
- it shows that valuation differences, where present, are concentrated among non-big firms rather than broadly across the sample.

## 2. What Now Looks Convincing

### A. Measurement

The audited construct remains the paper's biggest asset.

`PatentMismatch` is no longer carrying the whole paper by itself, but it does survive the stress test that matters most: when compared against nearby construct variants, it still looks like a legitimate main-text measure rather than a fragile arbitrary choice.

### B. Construct Validation

The later-realization results now give the paper a stronger backbone than the market section.

What is persuasive here is not that every nearby construct is significant. It is that the canonical construct continues to line up with later AI-realization outcomes in an interpretable way, while some variants either dilute the story or become too mechanical.

### C. Real Consequences

This is the strongest new results block.

Within the AI-talking sample, the paper now has a coherent delayed-consequences pattern:
- lower `ROA` at `t+2`;
- higher `R&D/assets` at `t+2`;
- much weaker evidence on sales growth and CAPX.

That looks like a plausible "costly catch-up" or "weak realized capability followed by later effort" pattern. It is far more credible than a sweeping delayed-correction return story.

### D. Valuation / Incentives

The refreshed financing/valuation lane is not a headline contribution, but it is useful.

The important read is:
- full-sample valuation and issuance-style financing are mostly null;
- non-big valuation differences remain visible;
- issuance-style outcomes stay weak in the current panel.

That is still informative. It says the signal is concentrated where valuation is harder, while the paper should not overclaim a broad financing-response mechanism.

## 3. What Still Looks Vulnerable

### A. Strong Market-Inefficiency Language

This still does not clear the bar.

The paper should not claim:
- market underreaction to AI-washing;
- delayed market correction of AI-washing;
- robust mispricing identified by `PatentMismatch`.

The market package now reads as mixed:
- some equal-weight portfolio evidence survives;
- stricter matched, benchmarked, and controlled designs do not give a clean standalone pricing result.

That mixed pattern belongs in the paper, but later and with narrower language.

### B. Governance / Institutional Mechanisms

The current panel does not yet support a true governance extension.

Without institutional ownership, analyst coverage, board structure, or richer executive-incentive data, a governance section would currently read as under-built rather than ambitious.

### C. Overinterpreting the Application Results

The application-side results are helpful, but they should be handled carefully.

They support the idea that some firms may later intensify formal activity after weak near-term grant realization, but that is not the same thing as proving that the initial disclosure was strategic misrepresentation.

## 4. Claim I Would Accept If I Were Reviewing This

A version I would view as defensible is:

"We develop an audited filing-based measure of AI-disclosure credibility. Firms flagged by this measure exhibit weaker near-term realized AI capability and later non-market outcomes consistent with costlier catch-up, while market responses do not cleanly distinguish these firms at filing or in stricter post-filing designs."

That is specific, defensible, and interesting.

## 5. Main-Text vs Appendix Recommendation

### Main text

Keep these central:
- measurement and auditability;
- descriptive rise of low-credibility AI disclosure;
- construct validation against later AI realization;
- real-outcome dynamics in the AI-talking sample;
- a narrower financing / non-big valuation extension;
- mixed market evidence, positioned later.

### Appendix

Move these here unless they become needed for exposition:
- full construct-variant screen;
- broader market robustness grids;
- data-gap notes for governance/institutions;
- market tables that mainly document nulls after stricter benchmarking.

## 6. What A Strict Conference Discussant Would Still Ask

Likely remaining questions:
- why should readers treat this as a disclosure-credibility paper rather than just a weak-capability paper?
- why is the non-market consequence story delayed to `t+2` rather than immediate?
- what exactly is the interpretation of higher later R&D: catch-up, late investment, or omitted firm type?
- if markets do not separate these firms, what should the paper conclude beyond "mixed evidence"?
- is one more external-data wave on governance or institutions worth the cost?

These are good questions. The current paper is now positioned to answer most of them honestly, except the last one, which still requires a strategic decision.

## 7. My Recommendation

Do not keep running more transformations on the same current market panel.

Instead:
1. rewrite the paper around the measurement + consequences spine;
2. keep the market section, but narrow it and move it later;
3. decide explicitly whether to invest in one additional external-data wave for governance / institutions;
4. if that data wave is not quick and credible, stop the empirical expansion and finish the paper around the stronger existing evidence.

## 8. Operational Conclusion

The paper is no longer in a "prove market inefficiency" phase.
It is now in a "lock the strongest claim, cut the weaker rhetoric, and decide whether one more external mechanism wave is worth it" phase.
