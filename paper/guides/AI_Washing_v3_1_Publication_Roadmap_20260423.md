## 1. Purpose

This roadmap replaces the earlier question

"Can the paper support a stronger market-pricing interpretation?"

with the now-better question

"What is the strongest, most defensible paper we can write given the evidence that actually survived?"

The answer at this point is no longer a broad market-underreaction paper.
The strongest version of the project is now a measurement, validation, and non-market consequences paper with a narrower, mixed market-evidence section.

## 2. Current Evidence Verdict

### What now clearly survives

- an audited filing-based disclosure-credibility construct;
- a sharp rise in low-credibility AI disclosure after 2022;
- construct validation against later AI realization;
- coherent non-market consequences in the AI-talking sample, especially lower `ROA` and higher `R&D/assets` at `t+2`;
- a non-big valuation difference as a secondary consequence result.

### What now looks mixed rather than central

- filing-date market differentiation;
- post-filing return drift as a clean pricing result;
- issuance-style financing responses.

### What the paper should stop trying to prove with the current panel

- robust market underreaction;
- delayed correction of AI-washing mispricing;
- a broad governance mechanism from currently unavailable fields.

## 3. Main Paper Posture

The paper should now be written around this claim hierarchy.

### Primary claim

We develop and audit a filing-based measure of AI-disclosure credibility, and that measure predicts later technology-realization and non-market consequences.

### Secondary claim

Markets do not cleanly distinguish higher- and lower-credibility AI disclosure at filing or in stricter post-filing designs.

### Tertiary claim

Where valuation differences appear, they are concentrated among non-big firms rather than broadly across the sample.

## 4. What Each Completed Packet Contributes

### Packet A: construct-variant screen

Keep as validation support.

Use it to say:
- `PatentMismatch` remains a credible main-text construct;
- `ApplicationMismatch` is a sensible appendix robustness variant;
- stricter variants do not obviously improve the paper;
- weak-patent-only results are informative but are not the same construct as disclosure credibility.

### Packet B: real-outcome dynamics

This is the strongest new main-text block.

Priority use:
- lower `ROA` at `t+2`;
- higher `R&D/assets` at `t+2`;
- weaker supporting evidence on sales growth and CAPX.

### Packet C: financing / valuation refresh

Use selectively.

Priority use:
- non-big valuation results;
- full-sample financing mostly null;
- governance explicitly deferred because the current panel does not yet contain the necessary data.

## 5. Main-Text Asset Ranking

### Tier 1 main-text assets

1. real-outcome dynamics table
2. core construct-validation table(s)
3. descriptive mismatch-incidence figure/table

### Tier 2 main-text assets

4. non-big valuation extension
5. short, mixed market-evidence section

### Appendix / robustness assets

- construct-variant screen;
- broader market robustness tables;
- Packet C data-gap note;
- any market tables whose main role is to document nulls or benchmark sensitivity.

## 6. Writing Sequence

### Results order

1. measure construction and auditability
2. descriptive rise of low-credibility AI disclosure
3. construct validation against later AI realization
4. real-outcome dynamics in the AI-talking sample
5. financing / valuation refresh
6. mixed market-evidence section
7. limitations and interpretation discipline

### Language discipline

Use language like:
- `does not cleanly distinguish`
- `mixed and benchmark-sensitive`
- `predicts later realization patterns`
- `consistent with weaker realized capability and later costly effort`

Avoid language like:
- `underreaction`
- `market correction`
- `mispricing identified by PatentMismatch`

## 7. Decision Tree For The Next Empirical Wave

### Option A. Stop empirical expansion with the current panel and move to paper completion

Choose this if:
- the goal is to maximize paper defensibility quickly;
- no high-quality external governance / institutional data can be staged soon;
- the current evidence is already enough for a coherent conference and submission draft.

### Option B. Run one additional external-data wave

Only choose this if the new data are both accessible and likely to sharpen the paper materially.

The best candidates are:
- institutional ownership / 13F concentration;
- analyst coverage or forecast dispersion;
- governance variables such as board independence, CEO-chair duality, or governance-score fields;
- richer incentive variables if there is a credible source.

Rule:
- do not open a new external-data wave unless it answers a question the current paper still clearly leaves exposed.

## 8. Conference-Facing Preparation Lane

Before any new data pull, complete this conference-defense package.

### Required artifacts

- compact referee-style memo
- updated rewrite notes
- revised roadmap
- clean main-text / appendix asset ranking
- one paragraph on what the market section now can and cannot claim

### Goal

Be able to explain in one minute:
- what the paper contributes;
- what the main evidence is;
- what the paper no longer claims;
- what one remaining extension would buy.

## 9. Immediate Operational Next Steps

1. revise the manuscript structure around the new results order;
2. move the strongest market tables later and soften the language around them;
3. decide explicitly whether to stop here or open one external-data wave for governance / institutions;
4. if no new data wave is approved, build the next paper revision packet and shift fully into writing and presentation mode.

## 10. Definition of Done For This Repositioning Phase

This phase is complete when:
- the paper has a settled claim hierarchy;
- the main-text versus appendix asset map is fixed;
- the market section is narrowed to what the evidence really supports;
- the team has explicitly chosen between `finish with current evidence` and `one external-data wave`.
