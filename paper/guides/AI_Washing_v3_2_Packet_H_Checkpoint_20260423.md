## 2. Why Packet H Matters

Packet H is useful because it gives the paper something the earlier broad market block did not:

- a public regulatory-salience event (`test_29`)
- a staggered firm-level financing incentive event (`test_30`)

So even if the broad market-underreaction story is mixed, Packet H shows that we did not stop at broad portfolio evidence. We checked two sharper event structures and then asked whether the market result changes inside one of them.

That is exactly the kind of follow-through a strict referee or conference audience would want to see.

## 5. Why This Is The Right Cut

If we put all three Packet H results into the main text as separate tables, the paper starts to spend too much space on a market block that is still not the strongest part of the evidence.

If we drop `test_29` or `test_30` from the main text, we lose two of the most persuasive signs that:

- disclosure quality responds to external scrutiny, and
- disclosure quality deteriorates around clear financing incentives.

That would weaken the overall paper more than it would save space.

So the right compromise is:

- keep the two strongest event-based non-market / quasi-external results in the main text
- keep the narrower market refinement nearby, but not as a full main-text centerpiece
